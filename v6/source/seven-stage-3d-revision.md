# Seven-stage 3D revision: The Ancient Machines That Moved Themselves

This is a production record for the user's reference infographic and camera request, not a final-master declaration. The historical machines and courtyard remain modern explanatory reconstructions, not measurements of surviving installations. Previously approved files and the private Drive review are not changed.

1. **Concept.** Stage the coin and other machines as three-dimensional objects in an inhabited architectural setting. Use faster, motivated push/orbit moves inspired by the supplied references, with calmer component close-ups so the action remains understandable. Retain the existing narration and spoken captions; no headings, reveal cards, or hands/fingers.
2. **Modeling.** Reuse the project’s independently created 3D mechanisms; revise the foreground chest with a decorated bronze relief, detailed planters and amphorae; add physical courtyard wings and an extended ground plane. These elements are meshes at different depths, not a still background plate. Later machine scenes have a visible supported steam hearth and new miniature theatre fleet, beacon, waves and storm props, treated as illustrative reconstructions.
3. **Texturing.** Use the cinematic renderer’s roughness/normal/metal responses and procedural materials. A newly AI-generated, non-reference, neutral limestone texture at `source/limestone-material.png` contributes subtly to the stone and masonry materials on real 3D geometry. Bronze, wood and water retain independent shader treatments.
4. **Rigging.** Give the coin mechanism an explicit parent/pivot relationship: the outlet lid rod stays vertical and meets the rotating beam’s left pivot. Add actual hinge-pin meshes parented to the two temple-door leaves; their movement uses their existing door pivots. The theatre’s handless sleeve/prop remains a staged mechanism. No humanoid skeleton is needed for this machine-only opening.
5. **Animation.** Evaluate the scene and mechanism rigs at 24 distinct time samples per second. Use accelerated world-space camera arcs/dollies through foreground, subject and background, with restrained moves around components. Coin landing precedes beam tilt; the lid responds to the beam. Temple, garden, fountain, steam and theatre beats have separately keyed causal stages (`film_sequences_v3.py`); the theatre's scenic change occurs behind shut doors. Water has changing 3D strand geometry, droplets and small ripples; fire uses animated 3D tongues. Static stills and numeric timing checks do not replace human playback.
6. **Lighting.** Use the HDR-based cinematic stage, a directional sun, higher-resolution shadow maps, subdued depth of field, atmospheric world-direction sky, and a practical light at the animated altar flame. Depth-aware camera-motion blur uses geometric reprojection rather than blending duplicate frames. Captions are applied after the picture/lens pass.
7. **Rendering.** `render_v3.py` renders native 1920×1080, 24 unique 3D frames/s with H.264. A separate v3 PCM retains the voice and user-supplied effects, with a few documented cue adjustments (`source/v3-soundtrack-adjustments.json`); the earlier approved sample and WIP remain untouched. Captions are from the existing approximate ASR alignment and require human timing review; color, action, camera movement, continuity and audio require real-time human playback before any final master. Source fingerprints and render/assembly receipts distinguish partial segments from complete work. No automatic upload/publication.

## Constraints and performance
A 1080p software-render benchmark on the available two-core sandbox yielded roughly 1.6 fps with the revised scene before the encoding overhead. A full 18:45.19 film at this setting is therefore a many-hour job and may be interrupted if the sandbox is reset. Render as checkpointed segments and validate every segment rather than replacing the uploaded 5:52 work-in-progress.
